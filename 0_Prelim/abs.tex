% ABSTRACT

Optimization theory is a cornerstone of machine learning. At the core of training deep neural networks lies the optimization of a loss function. However, despite the success of widely-used empirical optimizers and advancements in theoretical frameworks, a notable gap between theory and practice remains. This gap often arises from theoretical assumptions that do not reflect real-world conditions.

This thesis addresses these discrepancies through two main studies. The first study delves into non-smooth non-convex optimization by introducing a new convergence criterion that relaxes the standard notion of the Goldstein stationary point. It also proposes a reduction framework that converts online convex optimization (OCO) algorithms into algorithms suitable for non-smooth non-convex scenarios. This framework notably proves the convergence of stochastic gradient descent with momentum (SGDM) under these conditions. The second study focuses on optimization algorithms with differential privacy guarantees, highlighting the challenges and adaptations required to maintain privacy without compromising efficiency. It introduces private optimization algorithms applicable under varying convexity and smoothness conditions. Together, these studies deepen our understanding of optimization by developing new theories that better reflect practical conditions and address realistic needs, thereby bridging the gap between theory and practice.

We first present a \emph{differentially private online-to-batch} conversion (Chapter~\ref{chapter:dp-otb}). Any OCO algorithm with $O(\sqrt{T})$ regret can be converted in linear time into an $\epsilon$-differentially private stochastic convex optimization algorithm achieving the optimal convergence rate $\tilde O(1/\sqrt{T} + \sqrt{d}/\epsilon T)$ on smooth losses. This decouples privacy analysis from algorithm design and yields adaptive private algorithms whose rates depend on unknown gradient variance or parameter norms.

We then consider the non-convex, non-smooth setting under privacy (Chapter~\ref{chapter:dp-o2nc}). We give a zeroth-order algorithm that is $(\alpha,\alpha\rho^2/2)$-R\'enyi differentially private and finds $(\delta,\epsilon)$-stationary points with $\tilde O(d/\delta\epsilon^3 + d^{3/2}/\rho\delta\epsilon^2)$ data complexity, matching the optimal non-private zeroth-order rate; privacy can be ``for free'' when $\rho \ge \sqrt{d}\epsilon$. The algorithm combines the online-to-non-convex (O2NC) framework with a variance-reduced zeroth-order oracle and the tree mechanism.

Finally (Chapter~\ref{chapter:exp-o2nc}), we introduce an \emph{exponentiated online-to-non-convex} framework that extends O2NC with a relaxed stationarity criterion and gradient evaluations at iterates (no intermediate states). Applying this framework to online gradient descent yields an algorithm that is essentially stochastic gradient descent with momentum (SGDM) with an exponential random scaling, and achieves optimal convergence guarantees for non-smooth non-convex optimization---thereby bridging the gap between classical analysis, which assumes smooth or convex objectives, and the empirical success of SGDM in training neural networks.
